# Check-ins and duties

Read this before you act on a check-in report or add a duty.

What belongs in the plan, in a duty or on a risk page, how a duty run works, and the fix when the owner asks to fix plan and duties, is in [plan-and-duties](plan-and-duties.md). Follow that file first when the request is about the plan as a whole. In short, a plan step is a task that finishes, and keeping something working is a duty metric on a heartbeat.

1. On each duty's schedule, the server runs a code precheck and starts one duty job linked to this chat, named for the duty, the chat and the local time like `check-in frontend bro 14:00`, with the precheck as context. A check-in run with nothing due starts no job. The check-in runs every 120 min unless the owner set another interval on his page, and once a day while the plan has no open step (owner 10-22). Never set it under 120 min yourself, since only the owner sets a shorter interval (owner 10-15, c81 keeps this a rule in the brief, not a code refusal).
2. The check-in job pushes each open goal to its outcome for you. In a Prime browser chat, each run the precheck reads every open goal's number (its `target`, or the last run of a duty metric that a step under it fixes), the trend since the last check-in, the pace to the target, and the live job that owns the number. That job is the one on the goal's push step (a step under it with `duty: "checkin/goal_gaps"`), else a live job on any open step under the goal, with one on the goal's own duty metric first. When the number is off pace, the check-in job steers that owner with the goal's title, the owner's words for the goal, the measured gap and the ask to name and attack the biggest blocker. It quotes as the owner's words only lines from his messages in the chat, each with its message id, and never a board title, a requirement line he did not write or a metric name, and it reads the goal with `board.get` right before it sends, so the title and metric are the live ones. When no job owns it, the check-in job adds a push step under the goal and sets its job to a live job of the goal that already works on that blocker, or to one new job it starts with `rlm.create_session`, so a goal has one owner job and never a second copy. In sw factory the precheck reads each goal's target and its live owner job and keeps no trend or pace, so the check-in job steers or starts an owner only for a goal short of its target that no live job owns, and asks for a target where a goal has none. After the goals it pushes each open step toward done by resuming its job, starting a fix job, or closing it with evidence, and it moves each open item whose title reads like upkeep to a duty metric. In sw factory a step counts as moved only while its own job is at work: running a turn, asleep on a `board.wake`, or waiting on a working subagent. It counts as held only while an open For you todo links it by its step field or it waits on the owner's go. Every other open step is stuck, whatever its wait says (`checkInPrecheck` in sieun-pi `apps/chat/server/check-in-job.ts`).
3. Duty jobs apply their board ops to your board themselves and report what they applied, so never apply those ops again. Tell the owner only what matters, and leave the duty job alone, because once its turn ends the server measures the run again and archives the job.
4. A duty or check-in brief, a duty job's report and each server notice about them carry the headline number, at most 3 items in plain words and the path of a detail file, such as `~/.prime/agent/browser-chat/duties/<chat>/checkin-<run>.flagged.md`, that holds the full list, or `~/.pi/agent/chat/agents/<agent id>/check-ins/checkin-<run>.md` for an sw factory agent. Pass it on the same way in one short message, like "Instagram Tier A indexed 14.9 of 100%, about 10 days to target at this rate; the check-in started p71 index push on its biggest blocker; the rest are in <path>", and never paste the full list (owner 10-15: "it's not helpful at all").
5. Board upkeep is the check-in's own hidden duty, so never add a visible duty for it (c15).
6. Add or update a duty with `node --import tsx scripts/duties/add.ts <chatId> <duty.json>` in sieun-pi `legacy/prime/user-history`, where `<chatId>` is a Prime chat's session id or a pi agent's id or thread id, and leave out `schedule` so it runs once a day at a random free morning minute in the chat's priority band (c66). On 10-14 agents had set ops duties such as Engage reads to every 60 min and Watchlist shows green to every 120 min, and the owner said ops duties like CI review and crawler checks stay daily. Keep a duty daily unless the owner asks for another cadence.
7. Name a duty you add after the system it looks after, in two to four plain words, like "Chat system" or "Skills and harness", never a topic label like "Chat health" (c38).
8. A duty measures real outcomes, such as a thing that works or a mistake that stops repeating, and each metric measures what the owner's words ask for, with his limits as he states them (c67). On 10-14 the Engage reads duty counted pages read in 24 h (83,257 against 100,000) when the owner asked to "have the capacity to" read 100k pages a day, so the number swung with demand and our own caps. Its metric now reads healthy accounts times proven pages per account. The chat owns its duty file and precheck, and a duty run changes or drops a rule or metric that drifts from his words or makes no sense, and skips nitpicks (owner 10-19). A duty never counts the size of text such as words, lines or file size (c39).
9. Delete a heartbeat with `await rlm_heartbeat.delete(id)`. Keep a heartbeat only when it does work the check-in does not, such as a daily run.
10. The end state is every open goal with one live job that owns it, steered in a Prime chat whenever its number is off pace, which means it will not reach its target within 7 days at its rate, and then every open step moved by a live owner, held by an open For you todo that links it, or parked by an owner correction. A goal with no number gets a target from the owner's words. Each check-in brief opens with the goal count and the goals to steer or start, and the stuck count comes after them.
11. The check-in is judged on `goal_gaps` first, the open goals that no live job works on, and on stuck steps second, never on how many checks ran or findings got fixed (c64). sw factory records every check-in run, started, idle, skipped behind its last check-in job or failed to start, with these two counts under the built-in duty `checkin` in the agent's duties, and `GET http://127.0.0.1:5184/api/agents/<agent id>/check-in` lists them as `runs`. The rest of this item, `goals_off_pace` and pace, is Prime only. An off-pace goal that a live job works on is steered in each run and counted in `goals_off_pace`, which is not judged, because a goal is plan work that finishes and its pace is its own number on the board. Its line stays in the brief's goal section, and Flagged again leaves it out, because no board op clears it and its push schedule decides when to steer again. On 10-20 the Chat system duty read 11 of 12 check-ins red on such goals, such as Crawler VP's 200M creators at about 48 days to target. On 10-15 at 22:00Z the Crawler VP check-in reported stuck steps going from 3 to 1 while p71 sat at Instagram Tier A indexed 14.9 of 100%, about 10 days to target at its rate, and no job worked on its biggest blocker. The owner asked "are you pushing through on this? is it working?".
12. Every missed duty metric gets a fix step, a plan step with `duty: "<dutyId>/<metricKey>"` and a job that lands a code change for it. A duty job starts that job with `rlm.create_session` and, once it returns the session id, sets the step's `duty`, `job` and `sessionId`. When the step already has a live job, the duty job steers it, and a lookup of that job that fails, times out or finds nothing keeps it as the owner.
13. When a fix step closes, the server runs its duty again at once, a run with trigger `verify`, so the chat sees at once whether the land moved the number. When each metric the step fixes reads a time window that has not rolled over yet, no verify run starts, and the duty's next run after the roll measures the fix. While a duty stays red with no job at work on it, no fix step with a live job and no fix step held by an open For you todo, the server runs it again once per check-in interval, a run with trigger `push` that starts a duty job. Both hold in a Prime browser chat and in sw factory.
14. A metric that missed 3 runs in a row without moving toward its target is stuck, so change approach. A Prime duty brief says so, and an sw factory duty brief shows the streak as "missed 3 runs in a row". Start a new root-cause job that finds why the last fix did not move the number, give it a new step, and close or drop the old fix step with a note naming the new one. Running the same fix again does not count.
15. In a Prime browser chat, the server pushes the chat's jobs itself at each check-in. A job with no report that carries a number and no step change for one interval gets a nudge that says what it owes, and one quiet for two intervals or whose last turn failed is restarted with that message, and the chat gets one line about it. A job of the chat is the chat's subagent, so the check-in job steers it with `node --import tsx scripts/job-steer.ts <chat id> <job name or session id> <message>` in sieun-pi `legacy/prime/user-history`, which the server delivers, because `agent_message` from a check-in job cannot reach it (slack green guy 10-16: "No sibling matches"). Run those calls one after another, because on 10-20 the Crawler VP check-in fired about 25 at once with `asyncio.gather`, 9 came back 409 "This thread was closed while it was resuming.", and each of the 9 went through when rerun alone. sw factory's server pushes no job, so its check-in job nudges each quiet job itself with the job-steer call its brief names, `POST http://127.0.0.1:5184/api/threads/<work thread>/job-steer`.
16. A met duty metric closes its fix step only after it missed on an earlier run. On 10-16 frontend bro's p174 was flagged as done 4 times in a row because d1/walk_problems read 0 on every run, while its note said the walk missed the server's Stripe calls.
17. A check-in never steers a job to dispatch, cancel or redo a promote, or onto the promote or the us-metal01 box, because only the release owner runs promotes and the box owner runs us-metal01 (VP of CI, 10-20). On 10-21 the chat "mcp for chris" had to undo a check-in steer that told its job to cancel and redo promote 17. When a promote looks stuck, the check-in tells the CI and RepoOps thread with the run id, and a long `deploy prod` step is often a pgBackRest backup, as `repo/git-workflow` in auto-sns-agent says. Nor does it steer a job to cancel a running full staging proof (run-name `staging-proof <sha>`): a running proof runs to its end and the newest land's proof runs next, so a cancel only throws away specs. On 11-21 at 01:42Z a check-in called proof 44844933643 stale and steered alerts to zero 0115z to cancel it 38 min into its specs, and the agent gh shim now refuses that cancel unless PROOF_CANCEL_REASON names why. A proof queued behind another is a wait to report to the CI and RepoOps thread.
